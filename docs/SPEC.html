Personal finance, Malawi-first

Misi — water, for money that flows.

A personal budgeting and expense-tracking PWA built for how money actually works in Malawi: multiple bank accounts, Airtel Money, cash, unit trusts, and a US dollar account — all in one honest picture.

01 Vision

Buddy proved the model — wallets, categories, budgets, insights — but fails on entry speed, reconciliation, debt, multi-currency, and investments. Misi keeps what works and fixes the rest.

Entry speed above all

Logging a transaction takes under 10 seconds. Every screen is designed around reducing friction to log.

Reality-first

The app mirrors real money — 3 banks, Airtel Money, cash. When app and reality drift, reconciliation is a first-class flow, not an afterthought.

Allocation ≠ location

Where money sits (accounts) and what it's for (wallets) are separate dimensions, tracked independently.

Offline-first

Expenses happen in markets, buses, and shops — often without data. Everything works offline and syncs later via PowerSync.

02 Core Concepts

The vocabulary of the app. Everything else builds on these.

Accounts — where money sits

Real-world money locations: Bank 1/2/3, Airtel Money, Cash, plus investment custodians (unit trust funds, USD account). Each has a currency (MWK or USD) and an includeInNetWorth flag.

Wallets — what money is for

Virtual allocations, Buddy-style but unlimited: Spending, Savings (auto-filled from income), and one wallet per investment. Wallets don't map 1:1 to accounts — that was Buddy's friction — but every transaction records both its account and its wallet, so per-account expected balances are always computable.

Transactions

Three types: expense, income, transfer. Transfers are double-ended (bank → Airtel Money, savings → unit trust) and never count as spending. Investment purchases are transfers into the investment wallet.

Categories & subcategories

Unlimited, user-defined, with icon and color. Seeded with Malawi-relevant defaults — groceries, minibus, airtime & data, utilities, eating out, health — all editable and deletable.

Budgets, anchored to payday

Budgets per category/subcategory with an amount and optional rollover. The cycle starts on the 20th (configurable anchor day), matching when income lands — not the 1st. Weekly, monthly-from-anchor, or custom ranges. The budget view shows spent / remaining / pace.

Items — spend below the category line

"How much am I spending on milk a month?" shouldn't require a whole category. Every transaction has an optional items field — free text with autocomplete learned from your history. Typing "mi…" suggests milk from last time; one tap and done.

  • Aggregation is deterministic and offline: "Milk — K18,400 this budget period" is a simple sum, not an AI guess.
  • Categories stay broad buckets; items answer the specific questions. No category explosion.
  • AI arrives later (Phase 6) as an optional fuzzy-grouping layer on top — merging "Malaicha milk" with "milk", suggesting items from receipt text — but the foundation never depends on it.
Rejected alternatives: a category per product (unmanageable), or AI-only aggregation over a description field (fuzzy, non-deterministic, breaks offline).

Income sources & schedule

Income isn't one event — it's a rhythm. Misi models named income sources, each with an expected landing window:

SourceExpectedRole
Primary salary20thLargest — budget anchor
Secondary income24th – 30thExpected range, date varies
Allowance (primary job)10thLands mid-cycle
  • Expected-income checklist: each cycle, Misi shows which sources have landed and which are pending — a missing salary is visible immediately, and mid-cycle top-ups (10th, 24th–30th) are anticipated rather than surprising.
  • The split rule applies per source: each can have its own savings percentage (e.g. 20% of salary, 50% of allowance).
  • Budget pacing accounts for the rhythm — "you can spend K2,100/day until the allowance lands on the 10th."

Income split rule

"When income lands, move X% to Savings." Each income source carries its own percentage. Logging income makes Misi propose the savings transfer — one tap to confirm, editable. From Savings, a separate Allocate flow moves money into investment wallets. Mirrors the mental system: income → auto-save → deliberate investing.

Income→ Auto-save X%→ Allocate to unit trusts / USD

Investments

Each investment = wallet + metadata: fund name, type (unit trust / USD cash), optional units and latest unit price (entered manually — no market APIs for Malawian unit trusts). Tracks contributions, estimated value, and simple return. USD converts into net worth at a manually maintained FX rate with history, so currency gain/loss is visible.

Debts

Buddy has none; Misi does. Track money owed to you and you owe — person, optional due date, optional note. Lending = transfer to a debt wallet (spending money decreases correctly); repayment = transfer back. Optionally included in net worth.

Insights & overview

The free-Buddy gap — all computed locally:

  • Spending by category and by item, period-over-period comparison, trend lines
  • Spending calendar (daily heatmap)
  • Top payees, average transaction size, day-of-week patterns
  • Budget pace: "at this rate, Groceries runs out on the 14th"
  • Savings rate over time vs. the target rule
  • Net worth over time (MWK-converted), including investments and debts

03 Reconciliation — the killer feature

Every few days: "I have K X across everything — does Misi agree?" A guided flow, not a spreadsheet chore.

  1. Misi shows each account with its expected balance, computed from transactions.
  2. You enter the actual balance per account — checking each bank app, Airtel Money, counting cash.
  3. Per-account and total drift is shown. Green = matched, red = gap.
  4. For gaps, Misi helps close them:
    • Untracked-days heatmap — "you logged nothing on Saturday"
    • Suggested likely misses: recurring expenses, frequent payees, typical amounts
    • Quick-add missing transactions inline, pre-tagged with the session
    • Last resort: a clearly-marked balance adjustment so books always balance — the adjustment is absorbed by the Spending wallet, since untracked leakage is real spending
  5. Session closes with a summary. History is kept — drift-over-time is itself an insight ("you typically lose track of ~K3,000/week").
Reconciliation checks accounts against reality (the only ground truth). Wallets are virtual, so drift resolves into Spending by design.

04 Entry Speed — the manual-first design

OCR is deferred by choice, so the manual entry UX carries the product.

Always one tap away

A big "+" on every screen opens amount-first numpad entry. Smart defaults: last-used account/wallet, category guessed from payee history.

Recents, favorites, templates

Frequent payees and categories as one-tap chips (minibus fare, airtime, usual grocery run). Recurring templates (rent, subscriptions, tithe) pre-fill everything. Long-press a past transaction → "again today".

Offline by default

Draft transactions survive without connectivity and sync via PowerSync when back online. Entry never waits on the network.

06 Phased Roadmap

Each phase ships something usable on its own.

1

Core money mirror

Accounts, wallets, categories, transactions (incl. transfers), offline sync, net worth total.

Done when: you can log a minibus fare offline in under 10 seconds.

2

Budgets & income rule

Anchored budget periods (20th), category budgets, budget pace view, income sources with expected-landing checklist, per-source auto savings-split, allocate-to-investments flow.

3

Reconciliation

Guided reconcile flow, balance adjustments, reconciliation history, drift insights.

4

Insights & calendar

Overview graphs, spending calendar, category trends, savings rate, net worth history.

5

Debts, investments & multi-currency polish

Debt tracking, unit trust value tracking, USD wallet + FX rates, net worth composition.

6

AI capture (deferred by choice)

Receipt photo → cloud vision extraction → pre-filled transaction for confirmation, plus fuzzy item grouping ("Malaicha milk" ≈ "milk"). The receiptId and items hooks are already in the data model.

07 Deliberately out of scope (v1)

  • No bank / mobile-money API integrations — none exist for Malawian banks; manual + reconcile is the honest model.
  • No SMS parsing — iOS doesn't permit it.
  • No shared budgets or multi-person households.
  • No native app — PWA until the entry UX is proven.

08 Decisions locked

  • Reconciliation checks accounts against reality; deficits resolve into the Spending wallet via clearly-marked balance adjustments.
  • Net worth base currency: MWK, with USD converted in at the tracked FX rate.
  • App lock: passkey (WebAuthn) on top of better-auth — Face ID / fingerprint on open.
  • Item-level insights come from the deterministic items field, not per-product categories or AI-only parsing.
Misi — "water". Money flows; Misi keeps track of where it goes.
